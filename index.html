The academy

A real martial arts institute, not a fitness studio.

Markaz Martial Arts Academy was built around one idea — that a belt should mean something. The full World Taekwondo Federation syllabus runs at the centre of everything, with kickboxing, grappling and MMA layered on top for students who want to go further.

Head Coach AbdulSamad Khan opened the doors at Markaz-e-Khail-o-Saqafat in Gulberg Town so that anyone in Karachi could learn properly, from age five upward. Students grade honestly, compete nationally, and represent the academy internationally.

Your first 90 days

From walking in to holding your own

1
Week 1

Trial & fit

You watch, then you train. No fee, no kit needed — clothes you can move in and a water bottle.

2
Weeks 2–4

Fundamentals

Stance, guard, base and breathing. The unglamorous month that decides whether everything after it works.

3
Weeks 5–8

Drilling live

Positional rounds with a partner and a coach watching. Resistance rises only as fast as your control does.

4
Weeks 9–12

First grading

Full rounds, controlled — and your first honest belt test. By now you know exactly what to work on.

Under the Markaz umbrella

OUJ Performance

OUJ Performance carries its own identity and its own logo, but it is not a separate business — it sits under the same leadership as Markaz Martial Arts Academy, handling everything on the performance side of an athlete's development.

Where Markaz owns the syllabus, the belts and the technique, OUJ owns the engine: strength, conditioning, recovery, weight management and the gear that supports it.

Strength & conditioning — programming built around fight camps and grading cycles
Athlete testing — baseline testing, weight-class planning and recovery protocols
Performance supply — gear and supplements distributed through the Markaz store
Private coaching — one-on-one sessions for competitors and returning athletes
